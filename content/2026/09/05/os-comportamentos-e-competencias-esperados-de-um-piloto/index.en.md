When we think about “being a good pilot,” it is common to focus only on flying skills: how well a pilot performs maneuvers, takes off, or lands the aircraft. At first, that image of *Top Gun* comes to mind... But is that really all there is to it?

According to ICAO (the International Civil Aviation Organization), a proficient pilot is assessed based on a set of behavioral and technical competencies (Pilot Core Competencies), which are used as the basis for training and assessment under the CBTA model (Competency-Based Training and Assessment).

These competencies involve both technical and non-technical skills:

- **Knowledge (KNL):** It is the foundation for everything else. It is not about “memorizing for a test”; it is about having enough technical understanding to make good decisions, anticipate problems, and carry out procedures safely.
- **Communication (COM):** Sending and receiving information clearly, concisely, and without ambiguity, while prioritizing assertiveness with ATC and the crew (including non-verbal communication and confirmation or “readback”). In practice: standardized callouts, concise briefings, and confirming understanding (“Understood, adjusting…”).
- **Manual Flight (FPM):** Keeping the aircraft where it needs to be by hand: attitude, power, speed, altitude, and heading — with precision and awareness of energy. In practice: maintaining parameters in the traffic pattern, stabilizing on final, and correcting deviations.
- **Automation (FPA):** Using automation the right way: understanding modes, monitoring them, and anticipating what the aircraft will do — without “becoming a passenger to the autopilot.”
- **Situational Awareness (SAW):** “Staying ahead of the aircraft.” Seeing the whole picture: where I am, what is happening, and what might happen next. Anticipating traffic, weather, performance, fuel, threats, and trends.
- **Problem Solving and Decision Making (PTD):** Identifying a problem, assessing risks, choosing a strategy, and making a decision methodically, without rushing or freezing.
- **Workload Management (WLM):** Prioritizing tasks, distributing attention, using available resources, and keeping the flight under control even when a lot is happening. Remember: “Aviate, Navigate, Communicate.”
- **Leadership and Teamwork (LTW):** Creating a healthy cockpit environment through coordination, assertiveness, collaboration, mutual support, and a strong safety culture.

In other words, “flying well” is just one of the nine essential competencies for being a good pilot.
